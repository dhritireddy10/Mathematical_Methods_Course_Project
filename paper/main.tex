\begin{abstract}
This paper presents an extensive study on the application of TNRD for denoising grayscale images by removing multiplicative gamma noise. The model proposed in this paper consists of a sequential reaction–diffusion update where the filter kernels are static, having been randomly initialized, while the nonlinear effects and regularization coefficients are learnable.This paper compares the newly proposed TNRD model against a non-linear smooth diffusion PDE that has been specially designed to accommodate gamma noise. The experiment is conducted using a 400-image grayscale dataset, and it uses two levels of gamma noise, namely L = 1 and L = 10. The metrics used include PSNR and SSIM, alongside qualitative analysis and ablation studies. According to the findings, the proposed TNRD model obtains the best mean PSNR performance in terms of the highly challenging case of L = 1 compared to the PDE, showing superior performance by an approximate difference of 0.49 dB, while the PDE model performs slightly better than the TNRD model in terms of mean PSNR and SSIM for the less challenging case of L = 10. Based on the ablation tests, the TNRD model shows consistent superiority over the simplified models, proving the importance of the influence functions, filter banks, and the multi-layer design.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The problem of multiplicative gamma noise is an important one when considering image degradations where the intensity of observation is dependent on the signal magnitude. Multiplicative noise, unlike additive Gaussian noise, affects local contrast based on the signal magnitude and hence is challenging to handle. Conventional smoothing methods tend to over-smooth low-intensity objects or lose high-frequency components, while diffusion-based techniques are generally dependent on finely tuned hand-crafted coefficients.

The Trainable Nonlinear Reaction-Diffusion (TNRD) approach can serve as an ideal intermediary solution for model-based restoration and learning-based restoration approaches. Unlike the learning of the large general-purpose black box, TNRD models the restoration process as a series of diffusion steps that each have the ability to learn the nonlinear dynamics. At the same time, this approach allows us to keep the form of our variational/PDE-based restoration formulation.

The central idea of this project is to treat TNRD itself as the main research contribution and to compare it directly against a principled nonlinear smooth diffusion PDE baseline designed for multiplicative gamma noise. Not only does the purpose include determining if the model produces results better than the PDE model, but the effect of the different components of the architecture of the TNRD model will be analyzed using ablation tests.

The specific contributions of this paper are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item We train a multi-stage TNRD denoiser for multiplicative gamma noise reduction using the specified update equation with predefined random filters and learned nonlinear influence functions as well as reaction weights in scalar form.
\item Our proposed method uses a nonlinear smooth diffusion PDE, with intensity and gradient magnitudes used for guiding it. Our approach gives a better baseline compared to the use of general isotropic models or the popular Perona-Malik baseline.
\item The experiment carries out a comparison between two scenarios where the values of gamma noise are set as L = 1 and L = 10, employing 400 images from the grayscale dataset.
\item Experiments of ablation type have been performed to distinguish the effect caused by each parameter, including nonlinear influence functions, number of filters, and number of stages.
\item The current research can be summarized by a pipeline containing checkpoints, metrics, figures, and IEEE reports.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Image restoration with diffusion models has existed for decades. One of the most well-known techniques is anisotropic diffusion proposed by Perona and Malik, which showed that the smoothness process could take into account the edges in images by varying the level of diffusion based on gradient values of pixels \cite{perona1990scale}. More recent research made even clearer the link between restoration and reaction–diffusion models by proposing a TNRD model by Chen and Pock \cite{chen2017tnrd}. TNRD was especially attractive due to its interpretability and high restoration performance.

However, when it comes to multiplicative noise, the importance of PDE approaches cannot be underestimated, as they have natural capabilities for spatially adaptive smoothing, which can be easily implemented by using diffusion coefficients dependent on both image intensity and gradient magnitude. The latter makes sense for multiplicative gamma-noise and speckle-like corruption since it allows for different diffusion in uniform, edge-containing, and low-contrast regions. Hence, the use of nonlinear smooth diffusion serves as a basis for the proposed approach.

In terms of novelty compared to previous research, the paper does not introduce any fundamental theoretical model of diffusion. Instead, it proposes to use the TNRD framework for multiplicative gamma-noise under specific conditions – namely, that the filters remain constant, while the nonlinearity and regularization coefficients are to be estimated in a stepwise fashion.

\section{Problem Formulation}
Let $u$ denote the clean grayscale image and let $f$ denote the noisy observation. The multiplicative gamma noise model used in this work is
\begin{equation}
f = u \cdot n, \qquad n \sim \Gamma(L,L),
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the gamma noise parameter. The study evaluates two settings, $L=1$ and $L=10$.

The proposed TNRD model follows the stage-wise update
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tnrd_update}
u_t = u_{t-1} - \left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_k} \bar{k}_i^t * \left( \frac{u_\sigma}{M}\, \phi_i^t\bigl(k_i^t * u_{(t-1)p}\bigr) \right) + \lambda \left( \frac{u-f}{u^2 + \varepsilon} \right)\right),
\end{equation}
where $u_t$ is the restored image at stage $t$, $u_{(t-1)p}$ is the padded version of the previous stage output, $u_\sigma$ is the Gaussian-smoothed image, $k_i^t$ are frozen convolution filters, $\bar{k}_i^t$ are flipped filters used in the reverse diffusion operation, $\phi_i^t$ are learnable influence functions, and $\lambda$ is a learnable scalar regularization parameter. In the experiments, the gamma parameter $M$ is taken from the noise level used during training and evaluation.

These tasks can be summarized as extraction of linear structures through fixed filter banks, formulation of the nonlinearity using influence functions which are learned, and modulation of fidelity by the reaction term. Since the only things that get learned are the influence functions and the regularization functions, the whole process is consistent mathematically.

\section{Proposed Method}
\subsection{TNRD Architecture}
The proposed TNRD architecture uses $N_k=8$ filters with size $3\times3$ across five stages. Each stage is configured with its own fixed set of filters and its own influence function. The initial weights of filters are sampled randomly and remain fixed as required by the project design that requires $k_i$ be fixed while other parameters are learned on a per-stage basis.

In each stage, the influence function is defined as a grouped $1\times1$ convolution followed by a hyperbolic tangent activation function. This structure allows each filter response to be transformed individually through a trainable nonlinear function. The scalar parameter $\lambda$ is also learned individually for each stage, thus allowing the trade-off between diffusion and image fidelity to change across the stages of the diffusion process.

Gaussian blurring is used to define $u_\sigma$. Specifically, zero padding is performed before convolving the image with a Gaussian filter. The filtering operation in the forward pass uses \texttt{F.conv2d}, while the reverse diffusion passes use \texttt{F.conv\_transpose2d} with reversed filters.

\subsection{Stage-Wise Learning Strategy}
The network is trained in a stage-by-stage manner. After the training of a certain stage, it becomes fixed, and the optimization is carried out only on the current stage, while other stages are not updated. This scheme corresponds to the need to train non-filter parameters in a stage-wise fashion and improves the stability of the training process by preventing optimization from being performed simultaneously across all stages.

The loss function used is the mean squared error between the restored image and the original one. For training, we use the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $10^{-3}$, batch size of $1$, and $30$ epochs for the complete network.

\subsection{Novel Contribution}
The most important novelty of the present paper is the use of TNRD as the main learning-based restoration model within a multiplicative gamma model with a fixed filter bank and stage-wise nonlinearity learning. In contrast to conventional deep denoising approaches, the presented approach is based on a clear mathematical update equation and allows for interpreting the mutual interactions between diffusion and reaction processes. The above-mentioned innovation is also supported by a direct comparison with the nonlinear diffusion PDE, which is especially designed for multiplicative gamma noise.

\section{PDE Baseline Model}
The comparison baseline is a nonlinear smooth diffusion PDE given by
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \operatorname{div}\left(g(u_\sigma, |\nabla u_\sigma|)\nabla u\right),
\end{equation}
with diffusion coefficient
\begin{equation}
g(u_\sigma, |\nabla u_\sigma|) = \left(\frac{u_\sigma}{M}\right)^\alpha \frac{1}{1+|\nabla u_\sigma|^\beta}.
\end{equation}
This method combines gray level and edge steering. The factor $(u_\sigma/M)^\alpha$ controls the level of diffusion based on the intensity of the region, whereas the denominator controls diffusion in areas with large gradients. Numerically, the partial differential equation is solved by applying Gaussian blurring, directional finite difference, face-centered average coefficient, and explicit time integration with repeated border values.

This framework plays an essential role as the benchmark for its use of a mathematical approach to deal with multiplicative gamma noise. Therefore, any advance over the benchmark would be more significant than over any other denoising technique.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\subsection{Dataset and Split}
The experiment employs a grayscale image database that consists of 400 images residing in \texttt{FoETrainingSets180}  directory. These images are extracted using recursive identification from the data folder and divided into a training set of $80\%$, a validation set of  $10\%$, and a test set of  $10\%$, making a total of 40 images for the test set.

\subsection{Noise Settings}
Two multiplicative gamma noise settings are considered.
\begin{itemize}
\item Severe noise: $L=1$
\item Moderate noise: $L=10$
\end{itemize}
The above two environments provide a means to test the performance of the learned algorithm under conditions where there is significant and moderate levels of noise.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Performance will be measured using Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). The former measures distortion on a pixel-by-pixel level while the latter determines structural preservation and visual similarity. This will be done for the noisy image, PDE results, and our proposed TNRD results.

\subsection{Ablation Variants}
Four TNRD configurations are evaluated.
\begin{itemize}
\item Full model: $N_k=8$, 5 stages, nonlinear influence functions
\item No nonlinear influence: $N_k=8$, 5 stages, identity influence
\item Reduced filters: $N_k=4$, 5 stages
\item Reduced stages: $N_k=8$, 3 stages
\end{itemize}
These ablations reveal whether the proposed TNRD design choices are justified by the data.

The key observations made from the results include the following. For $L=1$, the proposed TNRD approach reaches PSNR of approximately 0.49 dB greater than the one of the PDE baseline. Thus, it demonstrates high capability of learning a nonlinear reaction–diffusion process when dealing with high-multiplicative noise. Still, PDE baseline shows a superior average value of SSIM, and hence the smoothing manually designed approach preserves better global structural coherence in comparison with the learning based approach.

For $L=10$, the PDE baseline remains marginally stronger, with an average PSNR advantage of about $0.25$ dB and a noticeable SSIM advantage. This implies that in case of mild noise, the manually designed diffusion scheme is highly competitive and the learned TNRD framework has not been able to learn any additional information to surpass the manual design.

It is worth mentioning that for both $L=1$ and $L=10$, the proposed TNRD model yields higher PSNR than the PDE baseline in 21 cases out of 40 images. In other words, although the proposed method is marginally worse in terms of average PSNR or SSIM, it is not uniformly weaker. On the contrary, it works well only for some images, which is in line with the local and nonlinear nature of the learned stage-wise updates.

The ablation study demonstrates its usefulness. Omitting the non-linear effect function causes the biggest drop in performance, particularly for $L=1$, which indicates the importance of the learned non-linearity for the TNRD algorithm proposed by the authors. Reducing the number of filter or stage sizes leads to an increase in degradation, thus implying the significance of both properties. Overall, it could be concluded that the entire proposed formula is not arbitrary and that each element plays its role in obtaining high performance results.

\section{Key Observations}
Several key observations emerge from the study.
\begin{itemize}
\item The suggested TNRD model proves itself to be competitive enough compared to the PDE-based benchmark by far, but not only because it performs better than the noise-filled image itself.
\item The model shows its greatest potential when the gamma noise gets severe; that is, its mean PSNR is higher than the corresponding value for the PDE-based benchmark.
\item The PDE-based approach proves to be very competitive too and sometimes even superior compared to the TNRD approach regarding mean SSIM.
\item Nonlinearity functions are critical since without them the results get substantially weaker, meaning that the learned nonlinear reaction is one of the key features that make the TNRD model perform so well.
\item Mean PSNR vs. mean SSIM shows that it might be possible to lower the distortion while not maximizing structural similarity.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Future Scope}
Despite the promising findings associated with the TNRD model presented here, there are certain aspects that need to be mentioned as potential drawbacks. Firstly, the model uses fixed random filters; therefore, its adaptivity to the distribution of the input data is questionable. The second drawback concerns the moderate underperformance of the proposed model compared to the PDE baseline in the case of a milder noise level. Finally, at present, the study can only be generalized to the grayscale image restoration case since the research is focused solely on one dataset.

Several directions are worth pursuing within future research projects. One option is related to the relaxation of the constraint related to the filter parameters being fixed in order to evaluate the possible improvements brought about by joint training of filters and influence functions. Another direction pertains to experimenting with stronger parameterization for the stages and increasing the number of reaction–diffusion stages. More generally, extending the current comparison to other data collections, considering additional multiplicative noise models, and focusing on color image restoration tasks would be beneficial.

\section{Conclusion}
An extensive experimental evaluation of the suggested TNRD model in denoising multiplicative gamma noise is carried out and the proposed model is compared against an established baseline based on a nonlinear smooth diffusion PDE. The TNRD framework follows a structured process of updates with predefined filters, learned nonlinear influence functions and scalar regularization parameters. Experimentation shows that the TNRD model exhibits the best average PSNR in the high noise case $L=1$, while the PDE model exhibits slightly better results in the low noise scenario $L=10$ and SSIM on average. Ablation studies show that using the full TNRD framework yields significantly better results compared to simplified variants, where the improvements are mostly achieved through the nonlinear influence functions. Altogether, these results show that by constraining the architecture yet allowing it to learn from data, the TNRD model can compete against a suitable PDE baseline, proving that the TNRD framework is a significant advancement in denoising multiplicative gamma noise in the highly noisy setting.
